# The strongest Candidate Verdict wins

Refutation judges one Author-Stated Open Problem against several candidate later papers, one
paper at a time. Each answer is a Candidate Verdict. The student sees one Verdict per problem, so
the several have to become one.

The rule is that the strongest claim wins. One Filled makes the Verdict Filled. Otherwise one
Partly Addressed makes it Partly Addressed. Otherwise the Verdict is Untouched. The reason is that
evidence of solving is positive evidence, while the other papers not solving the problem is the
absence of evidence. Five papers that did not address a problem say nothing about the one that
did. A rule that needed agreement, such as a majority, would report Untouched for a problem the
field closed, which is the output that sends a student into a dead project.

The cost sits on the other side. A single wrong Filled destroys a Gap, and the Gap is the kind of
Angle the product leads with. The guard is the quoted passage that the design already requires:
a Candidate Verdict of Filled or Partly Addressed carries a passage copied from the later paper,
and one that carries no passage is rejected and counted rather than aggregated. So a wrong Filled
has to be wrong and carry a real passage to do damage, which is a narrower failure than a wrong
Filled alone.

Two numbers make the risk visible instead of implicit. The count of rejected Candidate Verdicts,
and the share of Verdicts that rest on a single Candidate Verdict. The second is the number
Phase 1 has to improve, because a Verdict resting on one answer inherits that answer's error rate
whole.
